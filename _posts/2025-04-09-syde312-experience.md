**Context:** As a first year P.hD student at the University of Waterloo, I was given the opportunity to instruct 'SYDE 312: Applied Linear Algebra' for a cohort of 84 students during Winter 2025. This post summarizes my experiences as a first-time instructor, especially the lessons I learned and general thoughts on teaching. 

---
Just before the 2024 christmas break, while having a candid conversation with my former co-supervisor, Prof. [John Zelek](https://uwaterloo.ca/systems-design-engineering/profile/jzelek), the topic steered towards teaching. I remarked, "Teaching has always been an enjoyable experience for me as far as my retrospection goes. I truly believe that if I can explain things clearly, I've understood it well. Infact,I personally feel that I learn better when I teach". Prof. Zelek, after a brief pause, replied: "Well then, you are in luck! We need an instructor for a third year applied linear alegbra course in SYDE. Would you be interested?". 

This was an excellent opportunity for me, because -- (a) Ever since a very young age, I always enjoyed to teaching. I read Feynman's "Six Easy Pieces" and became a huge fan of simple explanations, and (b) I wanted to re-learn linear algebra from the fundamentals. So, without any second thoughts, I readily expressed my willingness. 

In the following 3-months, I gained invaluable experience on the ABC's of running an undergraduate course at a top-ranking university: from planning the course structure and coordinating administrative tasks to brainstorming new ideas to keep my class interested, engaged and curious to learn math, it was quite the learning curve for me. So, without more foreword, these are what I followed:  

- **Being explicit about my requirements.** On the very first day, 

- Food for thought 
- Intuition over definitions
- Flexibility with submissions 
- Fostering open discussions 
- Encouraging efforts over results. 






I hope this helps you in your teaching journey. Best of luck!
